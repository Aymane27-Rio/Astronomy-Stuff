01 / THE FUNDAMENTAL INTERACTIONS

Four ways the universe interacts

Choose a force to see what it acts on, how far it reaches, and the role it plays in the cosmos.

Gravity · the geometry of spacetime

General relativity describes gravity as the curvature of spacetime produced by energy and momentum. It dominates at astronomical scales, yet it is not part of the Standard Model’s quantum description. A complete, experimentally confirmed quantum theory of gravity remains unknown.

Electromagnetism · charge in motion

Electric and magnetic phenomena are two aspects of one interaction. Photons are the force carriers; because they are massless, electromagnetic effects can reach across infinite distances. This force binds electrons to nuclei and underlies chemistry, light, and most everyday contact.

Strong interaction · the glue of matter

Quantum chromodynamics (QCD) describes quarks interacting through gluons. The strong interaction confines quarks inside particles such as protons and neutrons. A residual strong interaction between protons and neutrons helps bind atomic nuclei.

Weak interaction · particles can change identity

The weak interaction can change one quark flavour into another. It drives beta decay and important reactions in stars, including steps in the proton–proton chain that powers the Sun. Its massive W and Z carriers make its range extremely short.

02 / ELECTROMAGNETISM IN FOUR LAWS

Maxwell’s equations

In SI units, these four compact statements link electric and magnetic fields to charge, current, and changing fields. Together they predict electromagnetic waves—including light.

GAUSS · ELECTRICITY
∇ · E = ρ / ε0

Electric charge is a source or sink of electric field.

GAUSS · MAGNETISM
∇ · B = 0

No isolated magnetic monopoles have been observed; magnetic field lines form loops.

FARADAY · INDUCTION
∇ × E = −∂B / ∂t

A changing magnetic field induces a circulating electric field.

AMPÈRE–MAXWELL
∇ × B = μ0J + μ0ε0 ∂E / ∂t

Current and changing electric fields both produce magnetic fields.

Symbols: E is electric field, B magnetic field, ρ charge density, J current density; ε0 and μ0 are vacuum constants. The displacement-current term is essential: it completes the law and allows electromagnetic waves to propagate through vacuum.

What’s missing?

Gravity is described extremely well by general relativity, but it has not been incorporated into the Standard Model. The model also leaves major questions—such as dark matter and the matter–antimatter imbalance—unanswered.

CERN’s Standard Model guide ↗

A mass nuance: the Higgs mechanism gives mass to elementary particles such as electrons and quarks, and to the W and Z bosons. Most of a proton’s mass comes instead from energy in the strong interaction binding its quarks and gluons.

04 / THE LANGUAGE OF FIELDS

Reading the equation

The Standard Model can be written compactly as a Lagrangian density. It encodes fields, their motion, and their interactions; the equations of motion follow from the action principle.

STANDARD MODEL LAGRANGIAN · SCHEMATIC
ℒSM = ℒgauge + ℒfermion + ℒHiggs + ℒYukawa

This is a map of the main ingredients, not the full expanded equation. Each term contains precise mathematical structure and constants measured in experiments.

Gauge fields

Fermion fields

Describe quarks and leptons and how they propagate and couple to gauge fields.

03

Higgs field

Includes the Higgs field’s dynamics and potential; its non-zero vacuum value enables electroweak symmetry breaking.

04

Yukawa couplings

Describe Higgs interactions with fermions; their strengths set the fermion masses after symmetry breaking.

Symmetry is the organizing principle. Gauge symmetries constrain the allowed interactions. The Higgs field’s vacuum state breaks electroweak symmetry, while preserving the mathematical consistency of the theory.

05 / THE OPEN FRONTIER

How far have we unified?

ESTABLISHED

Electricity + magnetism

Maxwell’s theory revealed these as aspects of electromagnetism.

ESTABLISHED

Electromagnetic + weak

Electroweak theory joins them in one framework; experiments confirmed its W and Z bosons.

UNCONFIRMED

Strong + electroweak

Grand unified theories propose a deeper connection at far higher energies.

OPEN QUESTION

Including gravity

A theory unifying quantum physics and gravity remains a major unsolved problem.

The search continues—not because the Standard Model failed, but because the universe still has more to explain.
